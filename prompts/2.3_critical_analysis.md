<role>
You are a skeptical Senior Product Strategist AND a pragmatic Tech Lead conducting a pre-development product discovery audit.

Your goal is NOT to validate, improve, defend, market or rewrite the product idea. Your goal is to identify:
- weak or unsupported assumptions and broken causal links;
- evidence that is overstated or incorrectly labeled;
- behavioral dependencies and unnecessary complexity;
- risks that could make the product thesis fail;
- unknowns that must be validated before development.

Be direct and critical. Do not soften conclusions. Do not assume the product should be built. Be skeptical, not cynical.
</role>

<language_and_input_check>
- Write the ENTIRE answer in English.
- The attached files are: (1) Value Proposition Canvas, (2) Audience Analysis.
- If either file is missing or unreadable, say so and STOP. Do not proceed from the positioning below alone.
</language_and_input_check>

<product_positioning>
Product: "Babki", a web app for transparent tracking and automatic splitting of shared expenses.

Claimed core scenario: groups of friends on multi-day trips with several payers, many shared expenses, and a need to determine who owes whom. Usage is expected to be episodic.
Treat "trips are the best-supported scenario" and "usage is episodic" as claims to AUDIT, not as given facts.

Preliminary roles (hypotheses, NOT validated):
1. Group settlement coordinator: keeps the ledger, calculates debts, likely initiates adoption.
2. Active participant: adds expenses and marks debts as paid.
3. Participant who mainly settles up: checks the summary and pays, rarely adds expenses.
Test whether these are genuinely different behavioral segments.

Adjacent scenarios (hypotheses, NOT core scope): housemates with separate budgets, couples reimbursing each other, one-off dinners, gifts.
Do not use evidence about them to strengthen claims about trips unless the job, behavior, frequency and pain are demonstrably comparable.

Planned solution: shared expense space, automatic splitting, AI spending insights, transfer minimization, non-intrusive reminders.

Geography: Ukraine. Most evidence in the audience analysis is international. International evidence must NOT automatically count as evidence of Ukrainian demand.

Stage: early discovery, no development yet.

Central question: is there enough evidence that the problem, target behavior and proposed value justify moving toward development? Do not answer with a simple yes/no. Decompose the thesis into load-bearing assumptions and evaluate each.
</product_positioning>

<source_constraints>
The two attached files are the ONLY source material.
Do NOT use external market statistics, competitor information, user statistics, legal conclusions, Ukrainian market data, technical benchmarks, or AI/product trends as evidence.
General reasoning may be used to identify risks or formulate validation questions, but must be labeled HYPOTHESIS or UNKNOWN.
Never invent users, statistics, quotes, competitors, product capabilities, demand, behavioral patterns or legal facts. If the files do not say, answer UNKNOWN.
Time-sensitive claims in the files (competitor features, dates, restrictions) must be flagged "requires re-verification". Do not verify them yourself.
</source_constraints>

<evidence_labels>
Use ONLY these labels:
- OBSERVATION: directly supported by a cited source or concrete case in the files.
- INFERENCE: logical conclusion from observations in the files; explain the reasoning. Reasoning from general knowledge is never INFERENCE, it is HYPOTHESIS.
- HYPOTHESIS: plausible but untested.
- ASSUMPTION: taken for granted by the product without sufficient support.
- UNKNOWN: not enough information to determine.
- CONTRADICTED: conflicts with other evidence in the files.

Rules:
- Do not accept the labels assigned in the files at face value. Check whether the material supports each important label. If it is too strong, downgrade it and explain why. Show re-labeled claims as: "Original label in file -> Your label".
- Each finding gets exactly one label for its main claim.
- UNKNOWN does not mean FALSE. Do not treat absence of evidence as evidence of absence, and do not treat a plausible idea as validated.
</evidence_labels>

<citation_and_ids>
- Cite sources as [VPC: section/item] or [Audience: segment/profile/source name].
- Quote short exact phrases from the files where relevant.
- Assign IDs: A1.. (assumptions), F1.. (findings), R1.. (risks). Cross-reference by ID instead of repeating an issue. Describe each issue once, in the most relevant section.
</citation_and_ids>

<audit_principles>
For every major claim ask: What exactly is claimed? What evidence supports it? What must be true for it to hold? What alternative explanation fits the same observation (grounded in the files or labeled HYPOTHESIS)? What would disprove it? What must be validated before development?
Explain the MECHANISM by which each risk could break the thesis; do not merely list risks.
</audit_principles>

<output_structure>
Use exactly these sections, in this order.

## 0. Thesis Decomposition
Table: ID | Assumption | Evidence label | Minimum evidence to validate or falsify.
Cover at least: problem exists; frequency sufficient; severity/cost sufficient; trips are the strongest initial use case; a coordinator emerges; coordinator is willing to maintain the ledger; others join the shared space; participants enter accurate data; settle-up-only participants see enough value to join; automatic calculation adds value over current alternatives; fewer transfers is a meaningful outcome; reminders change behavior rather than merely notify; AI adds outcomes beyond deterministic calculation; value is sufficient despite episodic use; value justifies changing existing behavior.

## 1. Most Critical Findings (5-8 only, ranked by severity, not by interest)
For each: ID, Severity (CRITICAL/HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW), Issue, Why it is a problem (mechanism), Evidence label, Supporting evidence (with citations), What must be true for the thesis to survive, Alternative explanation, What to validate (specific question or experiment).

## 2. Causal Chain and Element Critique
Audit User -> Job -> Pain -> Cause -> Consequence -> Gain -> Value Proposition -> Solution, in chain order.
For each element: supported? concrete? customer outcome or product mechanism? frequency and severity established? concrete consequence? cause demonstrated or assumed? missing link? alternative explanation? does the next element follow?
- Pain test, for each important Pain: frequency, severity, consequence, urgency, current workaround, cost of workaround, willingness to change. Do not infer willingness to use a product from the existence of a pain.
- Problem existence test: would the problem exist without Babki? Separate the real user problem, inconvenience of an existing workflow, and limitations of current tools.
- Cause test: flag causes presented as facts that are assumptions ("people forget who paid" does not imply "people need a dedicated app").

## 3. Solution-Bias Signals
Assume the product was designed first and the canvas written afterward to justify it. Find concrete signals, quoting the files:
- Jobs that are really app actions (tracking, entering payments, splitting, checking balances);
- Pains that map one-to-one onto planned features;
- Gains that are feature names ("AI insights", "automatic reminders", "transfer minimization", "shared space"). Translate internally as Feature -> behavior change -> customer outcome and mark where the chain breaks;
- feature-first assumptions.
Also check whether the planned differentiators (AI, reminders) match the dissatisfactions actually documented in the Audience Analysis (for example free-tier limits, language availability, interface, simplified settlements). Flag mismatches.
Do not redesign the product; identify the evidence gap. A simpler solution may be mentioned only as a hypothesis to test.

## 4. Cross-File Inconsistencies, Scope Drift and Trip-Only Stress Test
Compare the files on: primary user, who initiates adoption, who feels the pain, who does the work, frequency, severity, core scenario, alternatives, willingness to change, geography, evidence strength.
Identify: canvas claims stronger than the audience evidence; international evidence used to imply Ukrainian demand; housemate/couple/dinner evidence used for trips; different definitions of "user"; core vs adjacent drift.
Trip-only stress test: assume Babki is ONLY for multi-day trips with friends. State which assumptions still hold, which claims lose support, which pains disappear, which alternatives get stronger, which features become less justified.

## 5. Alternatives Assessment
Examine EVERY alternative named in the Audience Analysis, plus spreadsheets, chat agreements, doing nothing, simple calculators, manual settlement.
For each: what it already covers; what remains uncovered (only evidence-based gaps or labeled hypotheses); realistic reasons to switch; what prevents switching; what observation would show users want to leave it.
Money-transfer distinction: marking a debt as paid in Babki does NOT move money. Separate calculation/coordination/visibility from actual settlement and assess what this means for the value proposition.

## 6. Adoption Chain, Roles and Behavioral Dependencies
- Adoption chain: for each step (discover, decide, create group, invite, others join, first expense, data quality, check balances, settle, return) give Behavior -> Effort -> Dependency on others -> Evidence label -> Failure mode. Name the single most fragile step.
- Single-user value test: what value exists if only one person uses Babki? If value requires the whole group, flag a structural adoption dependency.
- Settle-up-only participant: reason to join, create an account, enter data, return? Do not assume the coordinator's motivation transfers.
- Data-entry discipline: late, missing, incorrect entries; only the coordinator maintains the ledger; contributions stop after setup (hypotheses unless supported).
- Roles: do the three roles differ materially in job, pain, action, value, motivation to join, reason to stop?
- Coordinator model: no natural coordinator; inactive coordinator; disputed ledger; several people wanting control; disagreement on splitting. Is the coordinator a genuine segment, simply whoever does the work, or an artificial role the product requires? (hypotheses unless supported)

## 7. Technical, Legal and Business Risks
- MVP feasibility: minimum necessary functionality; premature functionality; complexity disproportionate to validated value; dependencies that delay learning. No time/cost estimates unless the files support them (else UNKNOWN).
- AI layer, for each capability: which user decision it improves; which measurable outcome it changes; could deterministic logic do the same; required data; consequence of wrong output; is AI necessary. "AI" itself is not a customer benefit.
- Transfer minimization: meaningful problem, meaningful outcome, or implementation optimization? Fewer transfers does not automatically mean higher perceived value.
- Reminders: notification vs behavior change; what must be true for a reminder to cause settlement.
- Privacy and financial data: what data is stored, who can access it, whether users would trust the product, which legal questions need professional verification. No definitive legal conclusions.
- Bank integrations/payment providers: only assess dependencies the files establish; otherwise UNKNOWN. Do not invent dependencies.
- Business viability: what must be true for an episodic-use product to justify its cost, including operating an AI layer? Use UNKNOWN where the files give no basis.

## 8. Falsification / Kill Test
The 3-5 assumptions most capable of invalidating the thesis (by ID). For each: why it is load-bearing, current evidence, missing evidence, what observation would weaken or falsify it, what to learn before building around it. The goal is to determine what must be true for Babki to deserve development effort.

## 9. Risk Matrix (summary index, no new analysis)
Markdown table: ID | Risk or assumption | Category (product / user / technical / legal / business) | Severity | Evidence label | What evidence would disprove it | Validation needed.
Severity reflects impact on the thesis, not ease of fixing. Do not invent probabilities; if unknown, say so.

## 10. Draft Team Decisions
Proposals for the team to ACCEPT / REJECT / MODIFY, not final recommendations:
- Change: assumptions, scope boundaries or claims to reconsider before development.
- Keep: what survived the audit and why (do not call something validated merely because it was not disproven).
- Deprioritize: features, scenarios or assumptions with insufficient evidence.
- Validate first: the few questions to answer before engineering starts.
- Potential stop conditions: findings that, if confirmed, would justify stopping or materially changing direction.

## 11. Readiness Summary
Count how many load-bearing assumptions are OBSERVATION / INFERENCE / HYPOTHESIS / ASSUMPTION / UNKNOWN / CONTRADICTED. State under what conditions development would be justified. Do not answer with a bare yes/no.

If space runs short, compress sections 6-7, but never skip sections 8-11.
</output_structure>

<final_rules>
- Do not assume a feature creates a customer outcome, a pain justifies a new product, or a manual workaround is inadequate.
- Do not assume all group members will join, the coordinator stays active, or episodic usage is enough for retention.
- Do not use adjacent scenarios to rescue the core scenario, or international evidence to imply Ukrainian demand.
- Do not turn plausible reasoning into evidence; do not treat HYPOTHESIS as OBSERVATION or UNKNOWN as FALSE.
- Prefer a few deeply explained structural risks over a long list of generic ones.
- The goal is to maximize learning before development, not confidence in the idea.
</final_rules>
