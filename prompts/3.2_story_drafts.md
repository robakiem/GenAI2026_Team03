## 1. Your role and objective

You are an experienced product discovery facilitator, business analyst, and user story mapping practitioner. You are supporting **Babki**, a Ukrainian university student project: a bank-agnostic, mobile-friendly web service for recording shared expenses, agreeing on individual shares, calculating group balances, and coordinating settlement. **Babki does not transfer money.**

Create (1) a broad pool of draft user stories and (2) a structured **User Story Map**. This is **divergent product discovery**, not an MVP plan or an approved backlog: capture plausible alternatives, edge cases, trust and accessibility needs, and exploratory opportunities without imposing engineering feasibility or release constraints. However, distinguish research-grounded needs from hypothetical ideas; never fabricate evidence.

**Language:** Write the complete response in English. Generate the entire response exclusively in standard Markdown (.md) format, using only native Markdown syntax and elements; do not use HTML, SVG, Mermaid, interactive components, custom visualizations, or any other chat-specific formatting that may not render correctly in a standalone Markdown file.

## 2. Scope and product context

### 2.1 Exactly one target segment

**Target segment: friends sharing expenses during multi-day trips.** A group of friends travels together for several days; different people pay for transport, accommodation, food, activities, and other costs. Not every expense includes every friend. They need a transparent record of purchases and shares, then a checkable list of who owes whom after the trip, with actual repayment arranged outside Babki. The relevant journey may start before departure, continue during the trip, and end after everyone has settled or the group has consciously closed its records.

**Strict scope:** Generate stories and map the journey **only for this segment**. Couples, families, housemates, routine household bills, monthly household reconciliation, persistent rent tracking, and standalone one-off events are **out of scope**. A restaurant meal, shared taxi, gift, or other one-off purchase **during the trip** can be recorded as one trip expense; this does not make it a new target segment. Do not generate separate journeys or stories for other segments. Reusing an archived trip setup for a *future friends' trip* may be explored, but do not invent a general household-retention journey.

### 2.2 Behavioral roles within the trip group

These are **tentative activities within one segment**, not three validated market segments or necessarily separate accounts:

- **Coordinator:** maintains or checks the overall ledger, may organize invitations and reconcile final balances, and is most exposed when expenses are missing or repayments are delayed.
- **Participant:** pays for purchases and/or enters expenses, agrees on shares, checks balances, and may also act as Coordinator.
- **Settlement-only member (Payer):** primarily sees the summary and transfers their share externally; may never wish to register or enter expenses. This is a low-confidence behavioral hypothesis.

One friend can perform several roles, and roles can change. Do not assume every friend will join or regularly use the service. Consider both **collaborative entry** and **one Coordinator entering expenses for everyone**.

### 2.3 Starting product concept versus proposed additions

The original **product concept (not confirmed implemented functionality)** includes:

- Shared trip spaces and invitations; mobile-friendly web access without requiring everyone to use the same bank.
- Expense entry: amount, payer, optional category, selected participants, and equal or unequal shares.
- Automatic balances and debt simplification across multiple expenses and payers.
- A way to mark obligations as “settled”; proposed reminders and AI-based spending/category insights.
- Free, uncapped entry and a Ukrainian-language interface as intended properties.

**Identified gaps or possible additions, not existing capabilities:** view-only share links, guest access without mandatory account creation, Coordinator-only recording, a lightweight way to check and contest shares, edit history, duplicate detection, receipt attachment, contribution reminders, confirmation by the person receiving repayment, partial-payment statuses, copies of payment details or optional external bank links, export/archive, and reusing a group for a later trip. These are examples, not a mandatory feature list. Other ideas are allowed if their user value or hypothetical nature is explicit.

**Hard product boundary:** Babki can calculate and display debts, coordinate requests, and record users' reports or confirmations of payment. It **cannot itself move money or verify bank transactions**. Repayment happens in a bank app, by cash, or through another outside method. “Marked as paid” is not equivalent to “the recipient received funds.” Do not silently introduce an integrated payment processor, banking data access, guaranteed transaction verification, or legal compliance claims. Such concepts may be identified only as explicitly speculative, out-of-bound extensions.

### 2.4 Jobs to be done (the trip segment only)

- **T-J1:** Finish the trip with a correct, understandable, checkable answer to “who owes whom.”
- **T-J2:** Capture numerous expenses paid by different people while their details are still available.
- **T-J3:** Establish and apply fair, agreed shares for expenses involving different subsets of friends.
- **T-J4:** Coordinate external reimbursement without repeatedly or awkwardly chasing friends.
- **T-J5:** Enjoy the trip rather than making one friend an unpaid group accountant.
- **T-J6:** Let low-engagement members understand their individual obligation with minimal effort.

### 2.5 Pains: concrete obstacles and negative consequences

Interpret these as tentative customer problems, not automatically validated facts. When a pain is rooted in a competitor limitation, explain the real-world human consequence rather than treating the limitation itself as sufficient evidence.

- **T-P1 — Interrupted capture:** Caps, paywalls, or cumbersome entry can prevent recording several purchases promptly, leading to forgotten or incomplete transactions. Exact competitor free-tier limits are disputed; do not present one number as verified.
- **T-P2 — Uneven participation:** Some friends may not open the service or enter purchases, leaving the Coordinator to reconstruct amounts from chats, memory, and receipts.
- **T-P3 — Bank mismatch:** A tool that requires all members to use one bank may not fit a group with different banking providers.
- **T-P4 — Complex or opaque reconciliation:** Many payers and separate expense splits make the final obligations hard to calculate. Simplified transfers may save steps but can also be confusing or undesirable unless the underlying amounts remain understandable.
- **T-P5 — Awkward or missing repayment:** Asking for money can be uncomfortable; friends may pay late or not at all. An accurate calculation does not guarantee money arrives.
- **T-P6 — Friction for occasional users:** Mandatory account creation, complicated interfaces, or inadequate language accessibility in some tools may discourage friends who only want to see a summary and pay.
- **T-P7 — Uncertain records and fairness:** Forgotten transactions, incorrect included participants, mistaken shares, or disputed edits may create rework and mistrust. The prevalence of actual disputes has not been demonstrated.
- **T-P8 — Unreliable or misleading state:** Synchronization problems or ambiguous “settled” statuses may make people unsure whether the ledger is current or whether repayment really occurred.

### 2.6 Gains: desired positive outcomes, separately from pains

A gain is a **valuable outcome for a person**, not just a named feature or the inverse of a problem. If a gain partially overlaps with pain relief, acknowledge that overlap instead of inventing a different benefit.

- **T-G1 — Effortless trip-wide capture:** Log expenses as needed without arbitrary interruptions or charges for basic use.
- **T-G2 — Low-friction individual clarity:** Check one's share and proposed repayments without unnecessary installation, registration, or learning effort.
- **T-G3 — Shared confidence:** Obtain an understandable, reviewable record that friends can independently check rather than trusting one person's private calculations.
- **T-G4 — Settlement choice and transparency:** Understand a concise proposed transfer plan **and**, where useful, see how it relates to original obligations; minimal transfers are not universally preferred.
- **T-G5 — Inclusive trip coordination:** Bring together friends across different banks/devices, with accessible Ukrainian-language interaction, without interrupting the travel experience.
- **T-G6 — Social comfort and agreed fairness:** Feel that each person's participation and share have been acknowledged, and that asking for reimbursement is less personally awkward.
- **T-G7 — Optional understanding of trip spending:** See spending patterns by category if they help with a real decision; demand for category/AI insights is unvalidated.

### 2.7 Critical findings and uncertainty to preserve

The underlying research mixes product observations, international anecdotes, inferences, and hypotheses. It did **not** establish representative demand, prove the trip group's willingness to adopt Babki, or demonstrate that reminders or AI will improve outcomes. Some original Value Proposition Canvas claims were overconfident, and its intended features do not always match documented needs. Treat statements of fit as **hypotheses to examine**, not results to repeat.

- **F3 — Accounting is not actual repayment.** Separate calculation, communicating a debt, making a transfer externally, reporting payment, recipient confirmation, and unresolved disputes. A reminder or “settled” click cannot prove that money moved.
- **F4 — Participation bottleneck.** Model a group with active contributors, silent invitees, late joiners, and people who only check what they owe. Explore what works when one Coordinator enters everything and others never create accounts.
- **F5 — Episodic use.** A trip has a natural ending; explore clean closure, archival access, and optional reuse for a new trip without pretending recurrent engagement is established.
- **F6 — Need–feature mismatch.** AI insights and reminders are not validated differentiators. Do not build stories around technology for its own sake; always identify a real job, pain, gain, or explicitly labeled new hypothesis. Explain **pain relief** separately from **gain creation**.

Additional trip-specific edge cases worth considering without overstating evidence: members who join or leave during a trip; purchases involving only some friends; expenses paid in cash; refunds; partial repayments; accidental duplicates; edits after balances have been circulated; unexpected lack of connectivity; and different currencies. **Cash prevalence and multi-currency demand are unvalidated**, so tag any associated new needs as hypotheses.

## 3. Method for developing the map

Perform the following phases before presenting the final work.

### Phase A — Construct the journey backbone

Build a **chronological backbone of user activities**, not a list of app screens. Cover the complete multi-day trip experience: recognizing the need and agreeing to track; creating a trip space; inviting or informing friends; setting participation and split expectations; logging purchases as they happen; catching up on omitted entries; checking/correcting data; tracking interim balances; agreeing on a final picture; generating a proposed settlement; paying **outside Babki**; recording/confirming the outcome; resolving remaining discrepancies; closing/archiving the trip; and optionally starting another trip with the same friends.

You may refine or regroup this sequence. Show important **loops and branches**: logging throughout the trip, an inactive invitee, Coordinator-only entry, participants excluded from individual purchases, a disputed split, and a payment marked by one person but unconfirmed by another. Do not depict every activity as a mandatory step for every role.

### Phase B — Generate the broad story pool

For **each** backbone activity, identify meaningful **user tasks**, then draft fine-grained stories underneath them. Include the normal path, variations, errors, recovery, and social/contextual needs. Generate multiple viable alternatives where appropriate rather than prematurely picking one feature. Balance functional, emotional, social, mobile usability, accessibility, privacy, financial accuracy, trust, and transparency needs. Include starting-concept functionality, identified gaps, and clearly labeled exploratory concepts.

Aim for **approximately 45–70 distinct, nonduplicative stories for this one segment**, allowing more or fewer if needed for genuine coverage. Do not add filler to reach a quota, and do not truncate substantive ideas merely to hit the upper estimate. Do not prioritize MVP, estimate effort, assign engineering tasks, define releases, produce wireframes, or prematurely reject ideas for complexity.

### Phase C — Write valid user stories

Use exactly this form: **“As a [behavioral role], I want [specific capability or action], so that [user-valued outcome].”** Name **Coordinator**, **Participant**, or **settlement-only member (Payer)** as appropriate. Use a group member role only if the user-facing outcome truly applies to anyone. Each story should express one meaningful, testable *user outcome* rather than an implementation task. Split stories that mix unrelated intentions; avoid near duplicates and empty statements such as “As a user, I want AI.” A story may refer to an off-platform transfer only as a coordination or recording action, never as a Babki-processed payment.

### Phase D — Keep pain relief and gain creation distinct

For each story:

1. Trace it to relevant **T-J**, **T-P**, and/or **T-G** IDs. A story may be supported only by a job or be an exploratory **NEW HYPOTHESIS**; do not fabricate a mapping.
2. If it alleviates a pain, explain **Relief:** what harmful consequence changes, by what mechanism, and whether relief is partial/conditional.
3. If it creates a gain, explain **Creation:** what positive outcome becomes possible and by what mechanism. Do not simply rename the capability or repeat the pain-relief explanation.
4. Assign **Value type:** `Pain relief`, `Gain creation`, `Both`, or `Exploratory (job/new hypothesis only)`. If `Both`, write the two mechanisms separately. When a gain substantially overlaps with removal of a pain, state that plainly.
5. Assign **Origin:** `Starting concept`, `Identified gap/extension`, or `New idea`. Assign **Evidence status:** `Observation`, `Inference`, `Hypothesis`, or `New idea`. `Observation` may document what an alternative tool offers; it must **not** be interpreted as proof of user demand or of Babki's efficacy.

Do not claim that a capability completely resolves nonpayment, value-based disputes about fairness, or reluctance to participate when it only addresses part of the problem.

### Phase E — Consolidate and audit

Give all stories stable unique IDs (`US-001`, `US-002`, etc.). Deduplicate similar outcomes. Check that each appears in exactly one **primary map location** (one stage → one task) and in the complete story catalog. Other stages may mention cross-references without duplicating it as a new story. Check that all cited T-J/T-P/T-G IDs exist; mark genuinely new needs **NEW HYPOTHESIS**, not as research findings. Identify pains not sufficiently relieved, gains not credibly created, and capabilities whose relevance is speculative.

### Quality calibration examples

**Good — pain relief:** “As a Coordinator, I want to correct an expense and show the correction history to friends, so that we can resolve mismatched totals without reconstructing the entire ledger.” Link: `T-P7`. Relief: reduces rework and ambiguity; it does not guarantee agreement. Origin: identified gap/extension. Evidence: hypothesis.

**Good — gain creation:** “As a settlement-only member, I want to view the proposed transfers through a read-only link, so that I can understand what I owe without registering.” Link: `T-G2`, possibly also `T-P6`. Creation: gives immediate, independent clarity; separate relief mechanism, if claimed: removes the account-creation barrier. Origin: identified gap/extension. Evidence: hypothesis.

**Bad:** “As a user, I want AI spending analytics because AI is modern.” It names technology without establishing a user outcome.

## 4. Required output structure (entirely in English)

Provide every section below, in order.

### A. Framing and boundaries

Briefly state the **single target segment**, role definitions, product boundary regarding money transfer, evidence limitations, and that the output is an **exploratory story pool, not an approved backlog or MVP**. Do not discuss other audience segments or F1/F2.

### B. Trip journey backbone

Create a Markdown table: `Stage ID | Chronological user activity | User objective | Roles involved | Loop / branch / exception`. Include the entire pre-trip → during-trip → after-trip experience, through external repayment and closure. Stage names should describe what people do, not UI pages.

### C. User Story Map — primary artifact

Organize the map using **journey activity (horizontal backbone) → user tasks → story cards**. For each stage show its user tasks and then the **IDs plus concise titles** of stories placed below each task. Make a coherent text-based map that can be transferred to a visual board. Highlight branches, e.g., `Coordinator records alone`, `Friend never registers`, `Wrong shares corrected`, `Partial repayment`, and `Sender marked paid but recipient has not confirmed`. Reflect repeated expense-recording cycles within the trip. Do **not** draw an MVP or release cut-line.

### D. Complete story catalog and traceability

Show the **full text of every story** in Markdown tables grouped by the backbone stages. Keep these columns:

`ID | Role | Full user story | Job ID(s) | Pain ID(s) | Gain ID(s) | Value type | Mechanism(s) | Origin | Evidence status`

In `Mechanism(s)`, write `Relief:` and `Creation:` as separate clauses whenever both apply. Use `—` for absent links, and label genuinely new needs `NEW HYPOTHESIS`. Every catalog entry must be represented in section C. Keep story IDs consistent.

### E. Two independent VPC traceability audits

1. **Pain → response:** for **every T-P1 through T-P8**, state the underlying negative consequence, candidate capability/story IDs, and `Direct / Partial / Unaddressed / Hypothetical` fit. Explain the causal mechanism; distinguish a better ledger from actual reimbursement.
2. **Gain → creation:** independently assess **every T-G1 through T-G7**, giving the positive outcome, corresponding capability/story IDs, and the same fit labels. Explain gain creation without merely repeating the pain-relief rationale. Flag gains whose desirability is uncertain.

### F. Gaps, unresolved tradeoffs, and divergent possibilities

Identify unmet pains or gains; stories that depend on friends taking action; false payment-confirmation risks; interpretability versus minimum transfers; reminder consent/frequency; low-evidence AI ideas; and other creative approaches to the trip workflow. Distinguish **research-supported**, **inferred**, and **newly hypothesized** needs. Avoid competitor deep-dives and market validation.

### G. Coverage and consistency check

Report the **exact count of unique stories** and the **number assigned to each journey stage**. Verify that every story is present in both sections C and D with the same ID; each belongs to exactly one primary stage and task; T-P1–T-P8 and T-G1–T-G7 have been audited without invented evidence; Coordinator-only and low-engagement-member variants are represented; the journey reaches external payment, recipient confirmation or an explicitly unresolved state, and trip closure; and no story attributes real money transfer or bank verification to Babki. Correct inconsistencies before finalizing.

**Depth instruction:** The story map and complete catalog are the main deliverables. If output space is limited, compress prose and audits instead of omitting stories, stages, or traceability fields. Do not substitute a feature list for user stories.
