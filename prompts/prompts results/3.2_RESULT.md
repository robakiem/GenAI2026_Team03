# Babki — Exploratory User Story Pool and User Story Map

Target segment: Friends sharing expenses during multi-day trips Product: Babki — bank-agnostic, mobile-friendly shared-expense web service Method: Divergent product discovery, journey mapping, user story mapping, and Value Proposition Canvas (VPC) traceability Scope: Pre-trip preparation, expense tracking during travel, post-trip reconciliation, external repayment coordination, and trip closure

## A. Framing and boundaries

### A.1. Target segment and behavioral roles

This analysis addresses exactly one target segment: groups of friends sharing expenses during multi-day trips.

The same friends may contribute to transport, accommodation, meals, activities, and other shared purchases. Different people may pay, and individual expenses may involve different subsets of the group.

Three tentative behavioral roles are used:

| Role                           | Behavioral definition                                                                                                        | Important qualification                                            |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Coordinator                    | Organizes the expense record, coordinates participation, checks completeness, and may help reconcile balances and repayment. | Not necessarily a permanent administrator.                         |
| Participant                    | Pays for purchases, records expenses, reviews shares, checks balances, or participates in reconciliation.                    | Can also act as Coordinator.                                       |
| Settlement-only member (Payer) | Primarily wants to understand what they owe and arrange repayment externally.                                                | Low-confidence hypothesis; may never register or enter an expense. |

These are behavioral roles within the same trip group, not validated market segments or necessarily separate account types.

The map considers both collaborative recording and a Coordinator maintaining the ledger for friends who do not actively use Babki.

### A.2. Product and financial boundaries

Babki's starting concept includes shared trip records, invitations, expense entry, equal and unequal splitting, calculated balances, debt simplification, and a proposed way to mark obligations settled. Reminders and spending insights are also proposed.

These are conceptual capabilities, not confirmed implemented functionality.

Babki may calculate and present obligations, coordinate repayment requests, and record reported or confirmed outcomes. It does not transfer money, access banking transactions, or verify that a payment occurred.

The following states must remain distinct:

1. A debt has been calculated.
2. A proposed reimbursement has been communicated.
3. A person reports having paid outside Babki.
4. The intended recipient confirms receiving payment.
5. A payment remains unconfirmed or disputed.
6. The trip is closed, potentially with unresolved amounts.

Recipient confirmation represents a person's acknowledgment, not independent bank verification.

### A.3. Evidence and interpretation

The provided research combines product observations, anecdotal reports, inference, and untested hypotheses. It does not establish representative demand, adoption probability, or the effectiveness of a proposed capability.

The catalog uses four evidence statuses:

| Evidence status | Interpretation                                                                                                          |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Observation`   | Directly documented behavior or product characteristic. This does not automatically prove user demand or effectiveness. |
| `Inference`     | A plausible user need or consequence derived from the supplied jobs, pains, or gains.                                   |
| `Hypothesis`    | A specific desired behavior, interaction, or benefit that needs investigation.                                          |
| `New idea`      | A newly introduced possibility without supporting evidence in the supplied research.                                    |

The source material does not provide sufficient direct behavioral observations to mark individual proposed stories as validated `Observation` entries. Accordingly, the catalog uses `Inference`, `Hypothesis`, and `New idea` without manufacturing observational support.

Value types are assessed separately:

- `Pain relief`: Reduces a negative consequence or its likelihood.
- `Gain creation`: Enables a desirable positive outcome.
- `Both`: Contains separately identifiable relief and creation mechanisms.
- `Exploratory (job/new hypothesis only)`: Addresses a job or newly hypothesized need without claiming an established pain or gain relationship.

Where a gain overlaps with pain reduction, that overlap is acknowledged rather than treated as independent evidence.

### A.4. Status of this artifact

This is an exploratory pool of 69 draft user stories, organized into a User Story Map. It is not an approved backlog, implementation specification, MVP plan, prioritization, release sequence, or validation result.

No implementation feasibility, effort estimates, or release cut-lines are applied.

### B.1. Journey loops and non-mandatory paths

During-trip recording loop: S05 → S06 → S07 → S08 → S05. Purchases, adjustments, and verification repeat while friends travel.

Final review loop: S09 → S06/S07 → S09 → S10. Newly discovered information can invalidate an apparently complete ledger.

Settlement correction loop: S10/S11 → S07/S09 → S10. An edit after balances have been circulated requires updated proposals and communication.

Repayment loop: S11 → S12 → S13 → S11/S12. An unconfirmed or partial repayment remains visible until addressed or consciously left unresolved.

Low-participation branch: S03 → Coordinator-led S05/S06 → settlement-only access at S09/S11 → external payment at S12. Registration and active expense entry are not assumed.

Closure branch: S13 → S14 occurs either when repayment has been acknowledged or when the group deliberately closes an incompletely resolved trip.

S15 is optional. It does not imply that frequent or ongoing usage has been validated.

## D. Complete story catalog and traceability

### D.1. Reading the catalog

Each entry contains exactly one primary user story, written in the required behavioral-role format.

Traceability identifiers refer to the supplied trip-specific research:

- T-J1–T-J6: Jobs to be done.
- T-P1–T-P8: Pains and their negative consequences.
- T-G1–T-G7: Desired positive outcomes.

A dash (`—`) means that no defensible link is asserted. `NEW HYPOTHESIS` identifies a newly introduced need or assumption rather than an established research finding.

`Relief:` explains how a capability could reduce an adverse consequence. `Creation:` explains how it could enable a valuable positive outcome. The mechanisms describe proposed causal pathways, not demonstrated product effects.

## E. Two independent VPC traceability audits

These audits evaluate two different relationships:

- Pain → response: Does the proposed capability address the mechanism creating a negative consequence?
- Gain → creation: Does the capability enable a desirable positive outcome, even when no specific problem is being removed?

Fit labels describe conceptual fit, not empirically validated product–market fit or measured effectiveness.

`Direct` means the proposed capability targets the stated need directly. `Partial` means it addresses only part of the need or depends on conditions outside Babki. `Hypothetical` means the need–capability relationship is especially speculative. `Unaddressed` means no credible response has been identified.

#### Pain-audit conclusion

The candidate stories address identifiable aspects of all eight pains, but no pain is demonstrated to be completely resolved.

Three important residual problems remain:

1. Participation: A group ledger still depends on someone providing accurate purchase information.
2. Fairness: Correct arithmetic cannot settle disagreements about who should bear a cost.
3. Repayment: Recording and communicating debts does not cause real-world money to arrive.

The strongest conceptual distinction is between improving accounting accuracy and transparency and improving actual reimbursement outcomes. These should never be treated as the same result.

#### Gain-audit conclusion

The proposed map creates plausible routes toward all seven gains, but their credibility differs.

T-G2 and T-G4 have comparatively direct capability-to-outcome relationships: readable personal figures can support individual clarity, and visible alternative proposals can support settlement choice.

T-G3 and T-G6 depend strongly on human behavior: transparency may support confidence, and shared review may support fairness, but neither can be generated mechanically.

T-G7 remains especially speculative: providing spending information is not the same as establishing that people want it, understand it, or use it to make better decisions.

### F.3. Problems the story pool cannot solve by accounting alone

#### Uneven participation and Coordinator workload

US-003 and US-017 explicitly support one person recording expenses for the group. This could keep accounting workable when others are disengaged, but it may conflict with T-J5: enjoying the trip rather than acting as an unpaid accountant.

Possible divergent approaches include letting friends submit extremely lightweight purchase details, accepting end-of-day catch-up, or using a shared agreement that one Coordinator handles entry while others provide timely information.

None removes the dependence on friends supplying reliable information.

#### Mathematical correctness versus agreed fairness

US-018–US-020 make calculations more flexible. US-034 and US-035 allow objections and documented outcomes.

However, friends can disagree about the principle of an allocation even when the calculation is correct. For example, they may disagree about whether someone who missed an activity should contribute to a shared transport booking.

The system can expose assumptions, record discussion outcomes, and distinguish agreement from disagreement. It cannot determine the socially correct allocation in every situation.

#### False confirmation and the meaning of payment

The principal risk is collapsing different real-world states into a single Paid indicator.

An illustrative state progression is:

`Calculated → Requested → Reported sent → Recipient-confirmed`

This is not necessarily a linear workflow. A report may become `Disputed`, may represent only a partial amount, or may be corrected. Recipient confirmation can also be mistaken.

Appropriate wording should distinguish reported payment from recipient-confirmed receipt. Even mutual acknowledgment is not a verified bank transaction.

US-054–US-060 and US-062 provide alternative ways to maintain those distinctions, but no combination guarantees that external money moved.

#### More transparent settlement versus fewer transfers

Debt simplification is valuable when it reduces a burdensome series of payments. It may be undesirable if it directs someone to pay a friend who never originally covered their expense.

US-044–US-046 explore two different forms of value:

- Efficiency: Fewer external repayments to arrange.
- Interpretability and choice: A comprehensible explanation and a plan the group finds acceptable.

The research does not establish which preference dominates. Both should remain legitimate discovery possibilities.

### F.4. Additional opportunities requiring explicit investigation

These are discovery questions, not claims about demonstrated user needs.

Accessible mobile interaction. Would friends benefit more from larger touch targets, simpler text, screen-reader-friendly summaries, clear Ukrainian terminology, or personally tailored explanations? US-002, US-008, and US-042 provide starting points, but a mobile-friendly page alone does not establish accessibility.

Socially respectful accountability. Would a friend rather see a private unresolved amount, receive a neutral reminder, or have the Coordinator ask directly? It is plausible that avoiding reminders entirely could be preferable in some groups.

Checkable corrections after repayment starts. If a refund changes final balances after one person has already paid, should the group create a residual adjustment, agree on a new external repayment, or leave a documented exception? This is a new workflow hypothesis closely related to US-028, US-048, and US-060.

Privacy versus a durable audit trail. Some friends may prefer deletion of older expense information, while others may need preserved evidence to explain an unsettled debt. US-064–US-066 expose this tension without assuming a particular retention policy.

Low-effort contribution without regular participation. A person may be willing to send a receipt or amount but unwilling to navigate a shared ledger. The value of this intermediate involvement model remains unexplored.

Optional AI assistance. If people struggle to interpret many trip expenses, a concise plain-language explanation could be useful. But a simple category breakdown or transparent calculation may achieve the same outcome more predictably. No claim is made that AI improves reimbursement, trust, or engagement.

### F.5. Important unanswered research questions

The story pool leaves several questions open deliberately:

1. Do friends actually want a shared service throughout a multi-day trip, or do they prefer a single reconciliation afterward?
2. How often does one friend end up recording most expenses, and what support would reduce that person's workload?
3. Will settlement-only members open a read-only summary, or will they rely on a message from a trusted friend?
4. How frequently do incorrect splits generate genuine disputes, rather than simple corrections?
5. When a mathematically simplified plan differs from original payer relationships, do friends prefer fewer transfers or a more familiar repayment structure?
6. Would recipient confirmation meaningfully improve confidence, and would recipients consistently provide it?
7. Do reminders reduce repayment delays, or create additional social friction?
8. Are cash, foreign currencies, and offline connectivity common enough to materially affect the recording experience?
9. Is a trip archive useful after repayment, or does the group prefer to forget the accounting and move on?
10. Can spending insights inform a specific future trip decision, or would friends regard them as unnecessary analysis?

These questions are not ranked by delivery priority. They illustrate where divergent discovery should avoid turning plausible concepts into assumed facts.

### G.3. Final consistency observations

The resulting map provides broad coverage of the trip experience while preserving the most important distinctions in the supplied research.

First, recording is not the same as participation. A friend may be included in calculations without entering expenses or creating an account. This is essential to the Coordinator-only and settlement-only hypotheses.

Second, accounting correctness is not the same as agreed fairness. An expense can be arithmetically correct yet disputed. The map therefore includes allocation review, correction history, acknowledgment, and unresolved states.

Third, settlement calculation is not the same as reimbursement. The journey explicitly continues beyond generating a balance into communicating a request, arranging payment outside Babki, reporting the action, confirming receipt, and dealing with discrepancies.

Fourth, a trip has a natural endpoint. Archiving and optional future reuse are included without assuming that continued engagement is desirable or validated.

Fifth, speculative capabilities remain visibly speculative. Offline recording, multi-currency needs, cash-specific handling, detailed privacy controls, reminder preferences, and AI-assisted insights are explored without being presented as established demand.
