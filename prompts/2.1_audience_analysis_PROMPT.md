You are a UX researcher. Conduct desk research on potential Babki users using publicly available sources. The goal is to identify evidence-based audience segments, develop key user profiles, and recommend a priority audience for the MVP.

## Product context

Babki is a planned web application for tracking and automatically splitting shared expenses. A user creates a shared “space,” invites participants, and adds an expense: the amount, category, who paid, and whom to split it between. The system calculates “who owes whom and how much” balances and allows debts to be marked as settled.

Problem hypothesis: when sharing a household or organizing events, people lose track of expenses and debts, spend time keeping records manually, and feel uncomfortable reminding others about money.

AI insights, settlement suggestions that require fewer transfers, and automatic reminders are also under consideration. Their necessity and ability to reduce conflict have not been validated; do not use them as a basis for segmentation without evidence.

The initial market is Ukraine. Audience hypotheses: housemates, couples/families, and friends traveling or attending events together. Validate these hypotheses and consider other segments if you find evidence of a need.

## Tasks

1. Compare segments by the context and frequency of shared expenses, how they organize their finances, problem severity, current alternatives, and barriers to group adoption. Identify what specifically people find unsatisfactory about their current approach: calculation, data entry, agreeing on amounts, or repayment. If dissatisfaction is not substantiated, state this. Also investigate cases where precise tracking is unnecessary or the existing approach is entirely satisfactory.

   Initial alternatives to investigate: Splitwise, Tricount, Settle Up, Splid, spreadsheets/notes, messaging apps, and banking tools. Separately verify the current capabilities of monobank’s “Group Expenses” and payment splitting in Privat24. Distinguish between expense tracking, expense splitting, a shared fund, collecting contributions, and settling debts. The existence of an alternative does not mean that people use it or are satisfied with it. Consider multiple currencies, cash, and other contexts only where they materially affect needs.

2. Distinguish between segments and roles: who initiates adoption, maintains the records, adds their own expenses, or primarily settles up. Roles may overlap and recur across segments. Create separate profiles only where behavior and needs differ substantially.

3. For each profile, describe the context, role, current behavior, primary need, trigger, and barriers. Include demographics and attitudes toward reminders, privacy, or AI only where there is evidence of their relevance. Do not invent names or biographies; label unvalidated profile elements as hypotheses.

4. Compare MVP candidates by need severity, frequency, and adoption barriers, without numerical scores. Identify separately:
   - the best-supported use case;
   - a promising hypothesis for repeat use;
   - the recommended MVP priority and the rationale for choosing between them.

   Do not equate recurring expenses with recurring product use, or a better-researched segment with a more attractive one. If the choice depends on the MVP’s goal, provide a conditional recommendation; if the evidence is insufficient, do not select a winner. Assess accessibility for interviews separately, without assuming the team has access to the segment.

## Evidence requirements

Look for Ukrainian research, reviews, and public discussions, preferably from the 24 months preceding the request date. Use relevant public sources: app stores, forums, Reddit, DOU, public communities, and social media. For international data, explain the limitations of applying it to Ukraine.

Distinguish between actual experience, stated preferences, and product features. Use of a service does not establish problem severity or willingness to switch to Babki. Choosing not to maintain a detailed household budget does not necessarily mean there is no need to split individual expenses.

Account for conflicting evidence. Individual reviews do not establish prevalence; republished material does not constitute independent sources, and several comments in one discussion do not ensure sample diversity.

Label key claims, including those in tables and profiles:
- [Observation] — directly supported by a source, with a link and limits on applicability.
- [Inference] — an interpretation of the evidence presented.
- [Hypothesis] — an assumption to be tested.

For profiles and the MVP recommendation, state a confidence level — high, medium, or low — and briefly explain it in terms of the quality, independence, consistency, and relevance of the evidence. Do not automatically extend confidence in a use case’s existence to its priority for the MVP.

Cite only materials you have read that support the claims. In the source list, provide the direct URL and publication date, and, for key cases, the date of the experience described and geography, if known. Language, community name, or app store region does not establish an author’s place of residence.

Do not fabricate data or links. Distinguish between “source unavailable,” “no evidence found,” and “evidence of no need.” If web search is unavailable, state this and provide only preliminary hypotheses.

## Response format

In English, up to 1,500 words excluding the source list:
- Segment table — up to 6 rows.
- Key profiles — up to 3, using the specified fields and including confidence levels.
- MVP priority — comparison, recommendation, and its limitations.
- 3–5 major gaps: for each, provide a neutral question about past experience and the audience decision it would inform. Do not claim that qualitative interviews can estimate prevalence across the entire market.
- A brief list of what you searched for but did not find, and the sources.

Focus on the audience; the full Value Proposition Canvas and overall product analysis are conducted separately. Label all profiles as preliminary pending validation with real users.
